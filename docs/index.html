Cards With Cats

Four classic card games, one table, and three surprisingly strategic cats.

Download for Android Download for Linux View source
Version 0.4.2 · free and open source under the GPLv3 · no ads, no accounts, no network

Four games

Every mode plays against three cats who bid, block and hold back their high cards rather than dumping them at the first opportunity. Matches save automatically, so you can put the phone down mid-trick.

♥

Hearts

Avoid the point cards, pass wisely, and watch the running score beside each cat — or take all twenty-six and shoot the moon.

♠

Spades

Bid with a partner, manage your bags, and make the contract while keeping your opponents from making theirs.

♦

Oh Hell

Predict exactly how many tricks you will take as the hand size shrinks, grows, and the trump suit changes underneath you.

♣

Scum

Shed singles and matching sets to climb from Scum to President — then make the losers hand over their best cards next round.

At the table

The interface scales from a phone screen to a desktop Flatpak window.

Install

Downloads live on the GitHub releases page. Nothing phones home — the app has no network permissions at all.

Linux

Grab the Flatpak bundle and install it for your user:

flatpak install --user ./CardsWithCats-0.4.2-x86_64.flatpak
flatpak run io.github.crhy.CardsWithCats

Also available through Spaced Bazaar.

Android

Download the APK from the latest release and open it on your device. You will need to allow installs from your browser or file manager, since the app is not distributed through the Play Store.

Build it yourself

It is a Flutter app. With the SDK installed:

git clone https://github.com/crhy/CardsWithCats
cd CardsWithCats
flutter test
./scripts/build-flatpak.sh

New in 0.4.2

Gameplay, layout and bot fixes from the 0.4.1 playtest.

  • Tricks finish on screen. A completed trick now holds where it was played before being swept to the winner, so the card that ended it is always readable — and the score summary waits for the animation instead of covering the last trick.
  • No more auto-finishing. When the leader can no longer lose a trick, the hand still plays out card by card, the way it would with real people at a table.
  • Hearts shows the score beside every cat, with the current leader marked, so it is clear who is worth dumping points on.
  • The cats react as the round unfolds rather than only at the end — the new president grins, and everyone still holding cards is annoyed about it.
  • Smarter cats. The Scum bots keep a reserve of high cards in proportion to the low cards they still have to shed. Over 12,000 simulated rounds this moves their mean finish from 1.54 to 1.46 and drops last place from 27% to 23%.
  • Layout fixes across the board: role badges no longer cover played cards, mood bubbles keep clear of the ranks and the score card, and Hearts point-card tinting is a subtle wash instead of a solid block of pink.
